Connecting a neocloud to Dodona

Buyers pay Dodona for a cap, a floor or a fixed price on GPU-hours. The compute itself comes from you. This page explains, in plain words, what Dodona sends you, what you send back, and how the money is worked out. You can follow every message in the provider portal.

What a neocloud needs

  1. One webhook endpoint. An HTTPS address on your side. Dodona posts a JSON message to it whenever something about an order changes, such as a new reservation request.
  2. One API key. Dodona issues it to you once. You send it in the Authorization: Bearer header when you call Dodona back to confirm a reservation or report usage. You can replace it at any time from the portal; the old key stops working at once.

That is all. There is no SDK to install and no change to how you run your clusters. Each message carries an id, so you can safely ignore one you have already handled, and your endpoint answers with HTTP 200 to say it received it.

The life of one order

  1. Dodona to youreservation.requested means a buyer has paid. Please hold this many GPUs of this type, in this region, for this window.
  2. You to Dodonareservation.confirmed or reservation.declined. Confirming keeps the hold; declining releases it and the buyer is refunded through the escrow agent.
  3. You to Dodonadelivery.started means the window has opened and you have handed the GPUs to the buyer (or to whoever bought the hours from them).
  4. You to Dodonausage.reported says how many GPU-hours were delivered, once a day. For a full day that is GPUs × 24 hours.
  5. Dodona to yousettlement.computed means the window has ended (Dodona notes window.closed internally) and the result is worked out on the window average of the daily index.
  6. Dodona to youinvoice.issued carries the compute bill for the hours, at the price the contract says.
  7. Dodona to youpayout.issued confirms money released to you by the escrow agent, such as your premium share or the compute bill.

If the holder resells the hours before the window, Dodona handles the listing and bids itself (listing.created, bid.received) and sends you resale.completed with the new buyer. You deliver to them the same way; nothing else changes for you.

Amounts are US dollars rounded to cents; prices per GPU-hour have up to four decimals; every time is ISO 8601 in UTC.

How capacity holds work

  • You tell Dodona how many GPUs of each type you can offer in each region. The portal shows them as total, reserved and free.
  • When a request arrives, the GPUs it names are held at once, so the same capacity cannot be sold twice while you decide.
  • With auto-confirm on (the default), each request is confirmed automatically within the hour. With it off, the request waits in the portal until you confirm or decline.
  • Declining releases the hold straight away. The buyer's payment goes back through the escrow agent.
  • The hold ends when the window closes and the order settles.

How settlement on the window average works

Every contract settles on one number, the average of the daily index over the delivery window. A single expensive day cannot swing the result; the whole window counts. Index data is demonstration only.

Cap
The buyer pays the lower of the window average and the cap, per GPU-hour. That is your compute bill. If the average ends above the cap, the buyer saves the difference; as the seller of the cap you were paid a premium up front for giving that up.
Floor
If the window average ends below the floor, the holder receives the difference per GPU-hour from the matched buyer pool. Neoclouds usually buy floors to protect income on idle capacity.
Fixed price
The buyer pays the fixed price per GPU-hour whatever the average does. There is no premium, only a booking fee.

Who gets paid what

  • Premiums split at payment, 85% to the neocloud and 15% to Dodona as the platform fee.
  • Compute bills are invoiced at settlement and paid one day later.
  • Everything moves through the escrow agent. Dodona never holds funds.
