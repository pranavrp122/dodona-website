Price protection vs reserved GPUs vs on-demand

There are three common ways to pay for GPU compute you will need later. They differ in what you pay now, what you are locked into, and who carries the risk when prices move.

When to use each

On-demand
Small or unpredictable workloads, experiments, and anything where a price move wouldn't hurt the budget much.
Reserved capacity
Steady workloads that run all year, such as production inference, where you are confident you will use every hour.
Price cap
A planned run where you want protection from rises but think prices might fall, or where the size or timing might change.

The same run, with and without a cap

Take 100 H100 GPU-hours next month, with today's rate at $3.00 an hour. A cap at $3.00 costs $20 upfront. Here is the total in three outcomes.

OutcomeOn-demandPrice cap at $3.00
Price rises to $4$400$320
Price falls to $2$200$220
Run cancelled$0$20, less if resold

The cap costs a little when prices fall and saves a lot when they rise. That trade is worth making when a price rise would hurt more than the fee. Reserved capacity can beat both on rate, but only if you use every hour you commit to.

Figures are examples to show how the options compare, not quotes. Reserved-capacity terms vary widely between providers.
