What moves GPU rental prices

New chip generations
When a newer GPU ships in volume, rental prices for the previous generation tend to fall as capacity frees up. The newest chip is usually scarce and expensive at first.
Big model launches
Large training runs and popular product launches absorb capacity quickly, which can push short-term prices up across providers.
New supply
Data centres coming online add capacity in waves. A large cluster going live can pull prices down in that region.
Configuration
GPUs with fast interconnect (such as SXM with InfiniBand) cost more than PCIe cards, because multi-node training needs them.
Region
Power costs, data-residency rules and local demand make the same GPU cost different amounts in different regions.
Contract length
On-demand rates are highest. Committing to months or years buys a lower rate in exchange for paying whether or not you use it.

Reading a GPU price index

A price index averages rental prices across many providers each day, so it shows where the market is rather than one provider's list price. Two things to keep in mind:

  • Daily prices are noisy. A single day can spike or dip. For budgeting, the average over the weeks you will actually be running matters more than any one day.
  • The index is a benchmark, not a quote. The price you get depends on your provider, region, configuration and how many GPUs you need at once.

That is why Dodona caps settle on the average of the daily index over your window rather than a single day's price. The methodology has the details.

Budgeting checklist for a training run

  1. Count GPU-hours. GPUs × hours of the run, plus a buffer for restarts and evaluation. 64 GPUs for a week is 10,752 GPU-hours.
  2. Pick the window. The dates you expect to run. The further out, the more the price can move before you start.
  3. Price the range, not the point. Work out the bill at today's rate and at 20–30% above it. If the higher number breaks your budget, you have price risk worth managing.
  4. Decide how much risk to keep. Pay on-demand, reserve capacity or buy a price cap. The hedging guide compares them with worked examples.
  5. Check capacity, not just price. A low price is only useful if GPUs are available on your dates. Make sure your plan comes with access to the compute.
